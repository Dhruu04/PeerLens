Part 1: Getting Started

1. System Fundamentals & Pedagogical Foundations

PeerLens is an academic peer assessment system engineered specifically to resolve the persistent challenges of collaborative coursework in higher education. Group assignments foster teamwork, multidisciplinary communication, and project management skills; however, unmoderated group grading suffers from the "Free-Rider Paradox"—where hard-working students feel exploited by non-contributing teammates who receive the identical group mark.

Rather than relying on unweighted peer averages that introduce grade inflation or interpersonal friction, PeerLens employs the psychometrically validated WebPA algorithm (developed at Loughborough University) combined with Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales (BARS). This separates team-level milestone achievement from individual contribution, fairly scaling each student's final mark.

Zero-Account Friction
Students access evaluation portals through deterministic cryptographic tokens. No passwords, forgotten logins, or university single-sign-on friction.
WebPA Non-Linear Scaling
Normalizes raw ratings into proportional contribution shares, then scales individual grades relative to the group benchmark using configurable dampening.
Automated Collusion Auditing
Real-time heuristic scanner identifies lazy uniform grading, reciprocal inflation pacts between friends, and hyper-critical spite outliers.

4. Rubric Engineering & The IPAF Standard

The Integrated Peer Assessment Framework (IPAF) is an evidence-based evaluation model synthesized from the five most widely cited teamwork research instruments: CATME (Ohland et al., 2012), Salas's Big Five of Teamwork, the AAC&U Teamwork VALUE Rubric, the WebPA Loughborough Framework, and the Falchikov & Goldfinch Meta-Analysis.

IPAF balances observable task output with collaborative process behaviors, preventing students from rewarding popular but unproductive teammates or penalizing quiet, diligent contributors:

Dimension Evaluation Focus Weight Scale Behaviorally Anchored Indicator (BARS)
Contribution Quality Task Output 20% 1 – 20 Completed fair share of work accurately, thoroughly, and on schedule; high technical rigor and commitment.
Reliability Dependability 20% 1 – 20 Punctuality in meetings, proactive follow-through on deliverables without requiring peer reminders.
Problem-Solving Cognitive Process 15% 1 – 20 Resourcefulness under uncertainty; technical adaptability when plans shifted; unblocked struggling peers.
Communication Interpersonal 15% 1 – 20 Clear, timely asynchronous updates; active listening; respectful and constructive receipt of peer critique.
Initiative Leadership 15% 1 – 20 Stepped forward for challenging project modules without being asked; suggested constructive team directions.
Quality Drive Excellence 15% 1 – 20 Refused superficial completion; motivated teammates to elevate collective standards and final polish.

10.2 Excused Absence & Medical Exemption Architecture

During collaborative group work, students may encounter documented medical emergencies, bereavement, university-approved athletic competition, or late registration adjustments. If an absent student does not submit peer evaluations or receives artificial zero scores from teammates, traditional peer evaluation algorithms distort both the absent student's grade and their teammates' multipliers.

Academic Protection: Mathematical Denominator Neutrality

PeerLens features an academic-grade Medical Exemption / Excused Absence system that neutralizes the individual WebPA multiplier to exactly 1.000, ensuring the student receives the team's base assignment mark without penalty while isolating the team denominator so active teammates' scores are protected.

Algorithmic Exemption Neutralization
Rexcused = 1.000
Gexcused = Gbase · [ (1 − f) + f · 1.000 ] = Gbase
The excused student's unsubmitted status is omitted from team submission deficits, preventing artificial downgrade penalties across the cohort.

12. WebPA Scaling Algorithm Specification

The WebPA algorithm scales individual marks based on relative peer contribution. The mathematical derivation follows:

x̄ik =
1 Mi
∑j ≠ i sjik
Ri =
S̄i S̄team
Gi = Gbase · [ (1 − f) + f · Ri ]
Where f = 0.50 represents the balanced WebPA fudge factor, dampening extreme peer multipliers.

Impact of WebPA Stabilization Weight (f)

Fudge Factor (f) Weight Description Pedagogical Application
f = 0.00 0% Neutral (No Peer Effect) All students receive 100% of the team base grade. Peer factor adjustments are neutralized.
f = 0.25 25% Conservative Minor adjustments (75% baseline protection); suitable for introductory coursework or first-year students.
f = 0.50 50% Blended (Recommended) Standard WebPA balance: 50% group grade security combined with 50% peer differentiation.
f = 1.00 100% Pure Peer Score Full differentiation: peer multiplier directly determines individual mark. Used in senior capstone projects.

Complete Numerical Worked Walkthrough (4-Member Cohort)

Handling Missing Reviewers & Non-Evaluator Penalty

In higher education environments, occasionally a student fails to submit their peer evaluations before the cutoff deadline. PeerLens handles this through Loughborough's certified non-rater protocol:

  • Teammate Protection: The non-submitting student's column is excluded from the denominator. Their teammates are evaluated strictly based on the peers who did submit, ensuring innocent students are never penalized because an uncooperative peer went missing.
  • Automated Non-Rater Flag: The system tags the non-rater with an amber warning badge in the instructor gradebook.
  • Configurable Deduction Policy: Instructors can configure an automatic deduction (e.g. -5% to -15% penalty, or multiplier capped at 1.00 maximum) for students who fail to submit peer evaluations on time.

Scale Ceilings & Clamping Bounds

When a high-performing student in an excellent group receives a very high WebPA multiplier (e.g. R = 1.25 on a base mark of 88), the raw computation would yield 88 \times 1.25 = 110.0. PeerLens automatically applies safe boundary clamping:

Gfinal = min( TargetScaleMax, max( 0.0, Gcalibrated ) )
Grades are clamped at the maximum allowable scale (e.g. 100.0%, 20.0/20, or 4.0 GPA) unless bonus extra-credit is explicitly authorized in workspace settings.

Frequently Asked Operational Scenarios

Q1: How should I handle a student who dropped out or stopped attending mid-semester?
Navigate to Section 1 (Roster), locate the inactive student, and remove them from the active team. If teammates have already evaluated this student, their evaluations of the departing student are safely preserved in audit records while the active team's WebPA denominator automatically rescales across the remaining enrolled students, ensuring remaining team members suffer zero mathematical penalty.
Q2: What is the recommended WebPA approach for 2-person groups (dyads)?
In a 2-person team, each student is evaluated by only one peer. A single disgruntled or biased student could disproportionately influence their partner's mark. In dyads, instructors are strongly advised to set the Fudge Weight slider to a conservative 15%–25% (f ≤ 0.25) or review qualitative comments personally before releasing individual grades. For capstone projects, 3–6 students per group is the ideal psychometric cohort size.
Q3: How do I defend scaled individual grades during student grade appeals?
PeerLens equips instructors with an airtight audit trail. During grade consultations, open the student's Individual PDF Report Card or Johari Perception Radar. You can demonstrate that the final mark is not subjective instructor bias, but the normalized mathematical consensus of multiple teammates across five validated rubric dimensions, corroborated by time-stamped peer feedback comments.
Q4: Are peer reviews truly confidential, and can students see who wrote what?
PeerLens enforces a Double-Blind Confidentiality Protocol for students. When student report cards or feedback summaries are generated, all individual reviewer names and student IDs are stripped; qualitative comments are presented in randomized order. Only course convenors and authorized instructors in the Admin Dashboard have administrative privileges to audit who authored specific scores.
